# GP Operational Analysis

## Project Overview

This project explores operational activity within NHS primary care services using appointment data from NHS Hertfordshire and West Essex.

The analysis focuses on understanding appointment demand, workforce utilisation, appointment outcomes, access to care, and temporal trends in service delivery.

---

## Objective

To investigate operational patterns within primary care and identify key trends that may influence service performance and patient access.

---

## Business Questions

1. How are appointments delivered across different modes?
2. Which healthcare professionals deliver the greatest volume of appointments?
3. What are the most common appointment outcomes?
4. How quickly are patients accessing appointments?
5. How does appointment demand change over time?

---

## Dataset

The dataset contains aggregated NHS primary care appointment activity, including:

* Appointment outcomes
* Appointment delivery methods
* Healthcare professional types
* Waiting times
* Monthly appointment volumes
* Regional information

The analysis was conducted using aggregated appointment counts rather than patient-level data.

## Key Findings

### Appointment Delivery

Face-to-face appointments accounted for the largest proportion of activity, although telephone and online consultations also contributed substantially to service delivery.

### Workforce Utilisation

GPs delivered the largest share of appointments, while other healthcare professionals played a significant role in meeting demand.

### Appointment Outcomes

Most appointments resulted in attendance, although missed appointments and unknown outcomes represented a meaningful proportion of activity.

### Access to Care

A large proportion of appointments were delivered on the same day or within one week of booking, suggesting relatively rapid access to services.

### Temporal Trends

Appointment volumes demonstrated seasonal fluctuations, indicating periods of higher and lower demand throughout the study period.

---

## Conclusion

The analysis provides an overview of primary care operational performance and highlights important patterns in appointment delivery, workforce utilisation, patient access, and demand.

The findings demonstrate how healthcare analytics can support operational decision-making and service planning in primary care settings.
